I work on making machine learning models trustworthy enough to rely on — which usually means asking whether a reported number survives contact with reality. My interests sit at the intersection of explainable AI, deep learning, and large language models.

I hold an M.Sc. in Statistics (Data Science) from Tarbiat Modares University, where I ranked first in my cohort, and a B.Sc. in Statistics from Allameh Tabataba'i University, where I also ranked first. Research Interests

  • Explainable and trustworthy AI — feature attribution, the faithfulness of explanations, and how both degrade under distribution shift.
  • Deep learning for signals, images, and time series — EEG decoding, medical image segmentation, and anomaly detection.
  • Large language and foundation models — adaptation and transfer, retrieval-augmented generation, and hallucination detection.
  • Evaluation and reliability — whether reported performance holds up: benchmark saturation, leakage, and calibration under distribution shift.

A theme runs through most of this: I am generally less interested in proposing a new architecture than in checking whether an existing evaluation actually measures what it claims. Benchmarks saturate, attribution methods can be confidently wrong, and a reported score often rests on a split that leaked. Those failures are usually more informative than another point of accuracy.
